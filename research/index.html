Scientific Research and Engineering

Advancing the Engineering Science of Lunar Operations

Investigating the relationships among quality, verification, evidence integrity, autonomy, decision authority, and the operational challenges of sustained lunar exploration.

Explore Research Areas → Research Initiatives
Research Direction

Our Scientific Approach

Lunar operations introduce engineering challenges that extend beyond those encountered in conventional terrestrial environments. Communication delay, environmental uncertainty, constrained verification, limited intervention opportunities, and evolving operational conditions influence both system performance and the decisions that govern it.

LRI investigates these challenges through an interdisciplinary research program connecting quality engineering, systems engineering, reliability science, decision science, human factors, and aerospace engineering.

The research program concentrates on developing scientifically defensible methods for determining what operational evidence can support, when verification remains valid, how decision authority should respond to changing conditions, and how future lunar systems can achieve reliable and sustainable performance.

Research Architecture

Four Principal Research Areas

LRI organizes its scientific program around four complementary research pillars that connect fundamental engineering questions to practical lunar mission requirements.

PILLAR 01

Lunar Quality Systems and Mission Assurance

Developing quality architectures that connect engineering requirements, system reliability, operational risk, mission assurance, and engineering accountability across the lunar system lifecycle.

Research Topics
  • Unified Lunar Quality Framework (ULQF)
  • Risk-based quality management
  • Mission assurance architectures
  • Reliability and operational risk
  • Predictive quality and process capability
  • Engineering accountability and governance
Research objective: Develop integrated quality methods that support reliable engineering and operational decisions throughout lunar missions.
PILLAR 02

Evidence Integrity and Verification

Investigating the validity, traceability, temporal relevance, and decision-support boundaries of evidence used to verify engineering requirements and authorize operational actions.

Research Topics
  • Autonomous evidence integrity
  • Temporal evidence admissibility
  • Verification planning and verification logic
  • Critical-to-quality identification and structuring
  • Evidence readiness and qualification boundaries
  • Traceability and evidence-supported decisions
Research objective: Establish defensible boundaries for determining when available evidence can support verification, qualification, and operational decisions.
PILLAR 03

Autonomy and Decision Assurance

Examining how communication delay, changing hazards, aging evidence, uncertainty, and human-system interaction affect the allocation of decision authority during remote and autonomous aerospace operations.

Research Topics
  • Delayed supervision and temporal misalignment
  • Latency-aware decision authority
  • Decision freshness boundaries
  • Supervisory autonomy and authority transitions
  • Human-system coordination and situation awareness
  • Decision policies under uncertainty
Research objective: Develop analytical methods that help determine when supervisory decisions remain operationally relevant and when local autonomy requires different decision authority.
PILLAR 04

Lunar Manufacturing and Infrastructure

Investigating engineering methods for evaluating, verifying, and qualifying future lunar manufacturing processes, regolith-derived materials, and infrastructure systems under constrained operating conditions.

Research Topics
  • Lunar regolith manufacturing
  • Critical-to-quality characteristics and capability
  • Manufacturing process qualification
  • Testbed fidelity and qualification claims
  • Ground-based testing and computational simulation
  • Verification of lunar infrastructure systems
Research objective: Strengthen the engineering evidence that supports manufacturing qualification and future lunar infrastructure development.
Foundational Research

Unified Lunar Quality Framework

The Unified Lunar Quality Framework (ULQF) represents a foundational element of LRI's research program.

The framework connects quality requirements, verification, evidence integrity, operational timing, risk, and decision authority within the context of delayed lunar operations.

Related research examines individual engineering questions within this broader architecture, including evidence validity, verification planning, autonomous continuation, and the operational boundaries of supervisory decisions.

Scientific Methodology

How We Conduct Research

LRI selects research methods according to the scientific question, available evidence, operational context, and required level of analytical verification.

01

Structured Evidence Reviews

Systematic and scoping reviews, transparent literature screening, evidence extraction, methodological comparison, and research gap identification.

02

Computational Modeling

Mathematical models, discrete-time simulations, Monte Carlo experiments, sensitivity analysis, and comparative evaluation of engineering decision policies.

03

Historical Mission Analysis

Structured examination of historical mission records, operational evidence, communication events, engineering decisions, and human-system coordination.

04

Verification and Validation

Analytical verification, independent review where appropriate, reproducibility assessment, robustness testing, and examination of supporting evidence.

05

Framework Development

Development of engineering architectures, conceptual models, operational definitions, decision rules, and structured methods that connect evidence to engineering claims.

06

Standards and Requirements Analysis

Examination of applicable technical standards, quality management principles, engineering requirements, and qualification practices within the lunar operational context.
