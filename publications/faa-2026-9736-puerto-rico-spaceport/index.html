Puerto Rico Spaceport and Critical Launch Corridors

An evidence-gated strategy for evaluating Puerto Rico as a phased U.S. space transportation complex centered on Roosevelt Roads and Ceiba.

Author: Ralph Figueroa Posted: August 25, 2026 Comment ID: FAA-2026-9736-0003
Read the Official Comment Return to Publications

Core Recommendation

The Department of Transportation should advance Puerto Rico into a comparative, federally coordinated feasibility and launch-corridor study centered on Roosevelt Roads and Ceiba. The federal government should treat Puerto Rico as a phased space transportation complex, not assume that one proposed parcel already qualifies for a vertical launch pad. Construction support should follow only after the project proves safe launch geometry, credible operator demand, environmental and remediation readiness, resilient utilities, community acceptance, and a sustainable operating model.

Five Required Decision Gates

The comment recommends mandatory evidence gates before major public investment. Economic-development benefits should never compensate for unacceptable safety or environmental results.

1

Safety and Launch Corridor

Demonstrate usable mission corridors, debris containment, overflight exclusion, maritime coordination, emergency access, and acceptable risks to Puerto Rico and neighboring islands.

2

Credible Operator Demand

Require an anchor operator, defined vehicle classes, realistic mission cadence, payload requirements, and independent demand validation before major construction.

3

Environment and Land

Resolve remediation, land-use, wetlands, species, noise, plume, cumulative-impact, and community-participation requirements before selecting a launch-pad location.

4

Infrastructure and Resilience

Validate power, water, wastewater, communications, storm-surge, hurricane, seismic, emergency-response, and continuity requirements through engineering studies.

5

Finance and Operations

Release public funds by measurable milestones and require lifecycle cost, committed private capital, operating revenue, insurance, maintenance, and decommissioning plans.

A Phased Puerto Rico Operating Concept

The submission recommends a distributed space transportation complex instead of forcing every activity onto one parcel.

Ceiba Airport and Industrial Node

  • Horizontal flight, landing, testing, cargo, and emergency support
  • Payload processing, clean assembly, maintenance, and training
  • Range-control, data, tenant, and component-integration facilities
  • Connections to Puerto Rico's universities, workforce, ports, and suppliers

Vertical Launch Node

  • Compare remediated land-based and offshore alternatives
  • Use vehicle-specific trajectory and hazard analysis
  • Delay specialized pad construction until an operator freezes its interfaces
  • Preserve the broader Puerto Rico concept if one parcel fails the required gates

Critical Space Launch Corridors

Operational Definition

A critical corridor should function as a preplanned, performance-qualified airspace-management service for a specific family of trajectories and contingencies. It should not create permanently closed airspace or guarantee a launch slot.

Required Capabilities

FAA should use prevalidated geometry, common data standards, defined decision authority, near-real-time telemetry, dynamic hazard-area release, published service levels, and measurable effects on aviation and maritime users.

Why This Contribution Matters

Puerto Rico offers a geographically distinct, lower-latitude U.S. location with airport, port, industrial, academic, and workforce potential. The comment also identifies the evidence that decision makers must obtain before they commit public funding or select a launch-pad location.
