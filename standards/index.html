Technical Foundations

Our Approach to Standards

Established aerospace, quality management, systems engineering, and reliability standards provide essential foundations for the design, verification, qualification, and operation of complex engineering systems.

Lunar Research Initiative examines how these foundations can inform engineering methods for environments characterized by communication delay, limited intervention, constrained verification, environmental uncertainty, and evolving operational conditions.

LRI does not seek to replace existing standards. Its research investigates how established engineering practices can inform scientifically defensible approaches to emerging lunar operational challenges.

Research Application

Connecting Standards With Lunar Engineering

The following conceptual matrix illustrates how established engineering disciplines can inform LRI research questions.

Engineering Domain Reference Foundations Lunar Research Application
Quality Management ISO 9001 and AS9100 Integrated quality architectures, operational controls, process capability, and engineering accountability.
Mission Assurance NASA technical standards and ECSS product assurance references Assurance boundaries, system reliability, operational risk, and mission-specific engineering evidence.
Verification NASA and ECSS systems engineering and verification references Requirements traceability, CTQ-to-verification planning, evidence validity, and qualification claims.
Evidence Integrity Quality management, configuration management, and verification practices Evidence provenance, traceability, temporal relevance, and admissibility for engineering decisions.
Risk and Reliability ISO, IEC, NASA, and ECSS risk and dependability references Evolving hazards, constrained intervention, communication delay, and operational decision boundaries.
Manufacturing Qualification Aerospace quality management and process validation practices Regolith manufacturing, process capability, testbed fidelity, and supporting qualification evidence.
Supervisory Autonomy Systems engineering, human factors, and system safety practices Decision authority, delayed supervision, autonomous continuation, and escalation boundaries.

This matrix provides a conceptual research overview. It does not constitute a clause-level standards crosswalk, compliance determination, or demonstration of conformity with any particular standard.

Standards Authority and Institutional Independence

Lunar Research Initiative operates independently of standards development, certification, regulatory, and approval authorities. LRI research frameworks and technical resources do not constitute formal standards, certification requirements, or regulatory approvals. Users should consult the applicable official standards, contractual requirements, and mission-specific qualification procedures before making engineering compliance decisions.
